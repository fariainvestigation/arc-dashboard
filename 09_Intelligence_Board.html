ARC Geographic Profiling Protocol

Geographic Evidence and Movement Analysis

A defense-oriented protocol for testing location claims. It separates verified coordinates, approximate locations, coverage areas, inferred travel, and unsupported attribution.

Protocol status6 / 8STEPS DOCUMENTED
Required Analytical Protocol
1
Normalize time and timezone

Preserve source time, displayed timezone, normalized time, clock error, and uncertainty.

2
Classify every location

Exact coordinate, address, camera field, cell sector, general area, or analyst inference.

3
Separate person, device, and vehicle

Never treat a phone, account, plate, or vehicle as automatic proof of a person’s location.

4
Document source precision

Record GPS accuracy, tower-sector limits, camera view, ALPR lane, geocoder confidence, and gaps.

5
Test travel feasibility

Compare distance, minimum travel time, route conditions, stops, and impossible overlaps.

6
Identify exclusion and contradiction

Flag records inconsistent with the prosecution narrative and test alternative explanations.

7
Assess anchor and corridor hypotheses

Label spatial patterns as hypotheses unless independently corroborated. Avoid offender-profiling certainty.

8
Generate courtroom foundation

List custodian, system, retention, accuracy, authentication, hearsay, expert, and demonstrative issues.
